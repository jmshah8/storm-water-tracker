How this works

Every ten minutes we read each water company's published storm-overflow feed and record each discharge's start and end time. For each discharge we take the Environment Agency rain gauge nearest the overflow, within 10 km, that has near-complete readings. We add up the rain recorded on the day the discharge started and the day before. If that total is 0.25 mm or less, the discharge is flagged as a dry day spill under the Environment Agency's definition, and the evidence is shown on its page.
